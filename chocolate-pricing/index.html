01 / The question

Which retailer is cheaper? First, define “the same product.”

A direct price comparison can be wrong when listings describe different variants, pack sizes or purchase conditions. This analysis standardised prices to Australian dollars per 100g, reviewed product identity and kept a conservative matched subset.

Turn a pricing signal into a testable commercial decision.

07 / Reproducibility & limits

What the evidence can—and cannot—support.

The site deliberately keeps the caveats close to the findings. Expand the sections below for a concise technical audit trail.

Pricing basis

Prices are normalised to AUD per 100g. Recorded and conditional prices are separate scenarios. Conditional offers require qualifying purchase quantities and may not describe the effective price for every shopper.

Product matching

Matches were assessed from listing names, brand, variant and size evidence. The final export labels this as single-reviewer product-listing judgement; pairs were not barcode verified.

Machine-learning evaluation

The displayed figures come directly from the holdout evaluation export: 104 predictions, Random Forest R² 0.714, MAE A$0.65/100g and RMSE A$1.07/100g. A median baseline had negative R².

Inference limits
  • Cross-sectional snapshot, not a longitudinal price study.
  • No sales volume, stock availability or realised basket data.
  • No causal or sales-uplift claim.
  • Results describe retained pairs, not each retailer's full market position.
08 / The decision

Verify the condition, test the intervention, measure the outcome.

The project converts inconsistent competitor listings into a prioritised decision system. It shows where a retailer should investigate and test pricing or marketing action; transaction data and controlled experiments would determine whether that action increases sales or profit.
